# Recovery & Rest

## Importance of Recovery
- Muscles do not grow during exercise — they grow during rest and recovery.
- Overtraining without adequate recovery leads to fatigue, injury, and performance decline.
- Recovery includes sleep, nutrition, hydration, and active rest.

## Sleep
- Aim for 7-9 hours of quality sleep per night.
- Sleep is when your body releases growth hormone and repairs muscle tissue.
- Poor sleep reduces workout performance, increases injury risk, and slows recovery.
- Tips for better sleep: consistent schedule, dark room, no screens 1 hour before bed, cool temperature.

## Rest Days
- Take at least 1-2 full rest days per week.
- Beginners should rest more frequently (e.g., train 3 days, rest 4 days per week).
- Do not train the same muscle group on consecutive days.
- Rest days can include light walking, stretching, or yoga (active recovery).

## Active Recovery
- Low-intensity movement on rest days can aid recovery: walking, swimming, cycling at low intensity.
- Foam rolling and stretching help reduce muscle tightness and improve blood flow.
- Active recovery should not feel like a workout — keep it light and enjoyable.

## Signs of Overtraining
- Persistent fatigue that doesn't improve with rest.
- Decreased performance despite consistent training.
- Increased resting heart rate.
- Mood changes: irritability, anxiety, or lack of motivation.
- Frequent illness or colds.
- Chronic muscle soreness.
- Insomnia or disrupted sleep despite being tired.

## Recovery Strategies
- Prioritize sleep above all else.
- Eat adequate protein (1.6-2.2g/kg) and carbohydrates.
- Stay hydrated throughout the day.
- Consider foam rolling or massage for sore muscles.
- Cold showers or ice baths can reduce inflammation (evidence is mixed but some find it helpful).
- Track your training volume and fatigue to avoid overtraining.

## Deload Weeks
- Every 4-6 weeks, reduce your training volume by 40-50% for one week.
- Deload weeks allow your body to fully recover and adapt.
- You often come back stronger after a deload week.
- Signs you need a deload: plateaued progress, constant tiredness, nagging joint pain.
