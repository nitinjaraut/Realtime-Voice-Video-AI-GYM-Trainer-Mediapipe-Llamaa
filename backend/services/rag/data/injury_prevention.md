# Injury Prevention & Safety

## Warm-Up
- Always perform a 5-10 minute warm-up before exercise.
- Start with light cardio: walking, jogging in place, or jumping jacks.
- Follow with dynamic stretches: leg swings, arm circles, hip rotations.
- Do 1-2 light warm-up sets of the exercise you're about to perform.
- A proper warm-up increases blood flow, raises muscle temperature, and reduces injury risk.

## Cool-Down
- After your workout, perform 5-10 minutes of light activity (walking).
- Follow with static stretches, holding each for 20-30 seconds.
- Focus on the muscles you trained: quad stretch, hamstring stretch, shoulder stretch, chest stretch.
- Cooling down helps reduce muscle soreness and promotes recovery.

## Common Injuries and Prevention

### Knee Pain
- Common causes: improper squat/lunge form, knee caving inward, excessive forward lean.
- Prevention: strengthen glutes and hip stabilizers, ensure proper alignment, don't let knees overshoot toes excessively.
- If experiencing sharp pain, stop the exercise and consult a professional.

### Lower Back Pain
- Common causes: rounding the lower back during squats or deadlifts, weak core muscles.
- Prevention: brace your core before every rep, maintain a neutral spine, strengthen core with planks.
- Avoid exercises that cause pain. Pain is a signal, not something to push through.

### Shoulder Injuries
- Common causes: flaring elbows during push-ups or presses, lifting too heavy overhead.
- Prevention: keep elbows at 45 degrees during push-ups, strengthen rotator cuff muscles, avoid behind-the-neck presses.
- Warm up shoulders thoroughly with band pull-aparts and arm circles.

### Wrist Pain
- Common causes: improper wrist position during push-ups or curls.
- Prevention: keep wrists neutral (straight), use wrist wraps if needed, stretch wrists before and after training.

## General Safety Rules
- Never sacrifice form for heavier weight. Form always comes first.
- If you feel sharp or shooting pain, stop immediately.
- Rest at least 48 hours before training the same muscle group again.
- Get adequate sleep (7-9 hours) for proper recovery.
- Listen to your body. Muscle soreness is normal; joint pain is not.

## When to See a Doctor
- Persistent pain that doesn't improve with rest.
- Swelling, bruising, or visible deformity.
- Loss of range of motion.
- Numbness or tingling.
- Pain that wakes you up at night.
